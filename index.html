About Me

More than a degree.
More than a profession.

I'm driven by a need to understand mechanisms — not just to use them, but to see what makes them tick. Whether it's a neural network learning to recognize patterns, a mathematical proof revealing hidden structure, or a piece of code that behaves unexpectedly, I want to know why.

This curiosity has led me through mathematics, through algorithms, through late-night experiments that fail more often than they succeed. But each failure teaches something. Each dead end reveals a boundary worth pushing.

I don't claim mastery. I'm still exploring, still developing, still figuring out what kind of researcher — what kind of person — I want to be. What I do know is that I want to invent rather than only implement. I want to ask questions that haven't been asked yet. And I want the work I do to mean something beyond a line on a résumé.

How I think

Understand before building. Question defaults. Embrace the discomfort of not knowing. Let mathematics guide intuition. Stay willing to be wrong.

Research Interests

Questions I Want
to Explore

Novel Neural Network Architectures

Can we design architectures that learn more effectively — with better inductive biases, stronger generalization, and more interpretable internal representations?

Continual Learning

Can AI learn new concepts continuously without catastrophic forgetting of previous knowledge — approaching the plasticity-stability balance of biological intelligence?

Learning Efficiency

Can models achieve more with dramatically less — less data, less computation, less energy — while maintaining or improving capability?

Mathematical Foundations

Can deeper mathematical discoveries — in optimization, geometry, information theory — lead to fundamentally new learning mechanisms?

"I want to understand the mathematics behind intelligence and use that understanding to invent better ways for machines to learn."
